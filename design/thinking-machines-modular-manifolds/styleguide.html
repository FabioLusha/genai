This page is the design language explaining itself. Every heading, paragraph, table, swatch and note below is rendered with the same stylesheet as the essay — nothing here is a screenshot, and nothing is a special case. If a specimen looks right, the same markup will look right in a post.

The premise is simple: an academic paper rendered as a web essay. Prose is set in a book serif at a comfortable 17 px; every piece of apparatus — byline, captions, tables, contents, footer — is a quiet grotesque. Colour is almost entirely absent. Structure comes from one narrow column, generous space above headings, and hairlines rather than boxes.

Typography

Four families do all the work: two carry the voice, one carries data, and one appears exactly once. The contrast between the serif and the sans is the signature of the system — reading text is serif, apparatus is sans.

Iowan Old Style regular 400 · bold 700

Manifolds, optimizers, and the shape of a step

Reading text. Every paragraph, heading, caption body, quote and inline quotation. Fallback: Georgia, serif.

GT America regular 400

Metadata, navigation, captions, tables, footer

All apparatus. 15 px for chrome, 13 px for captions and tables. Never used for prose.

GT America Mono regular 400

Code only: inline snippets at 0.9 em, code blocks at 13 px/1.4.

The scale

Sizes are few and deliberate. Headings are sentence case, never letterspaced, and space above a heading is roughly three times the space below it — a heading belongs to what follows.

Inside a paragraph

Inline emphasis is restrained. Bold marks a term being defined and is never used for shouting. Italics carry the title of a work or a technical phrase. A link is the same ink as the muted text with a 30 % underline, and darkens on hover. Inline code looks like optimizer.step() — mono at 0.9 em in a muted red, with no background. A footnote marker arrives as a small raised digit,Like this one: the note lives in the right margin, in 0.9 rem serif, numbered once and never repeated. and mathematics sits inline as $w \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ without disturbing the line.

A display equation is centred, unscaled, and given its own breathing room: it is the only element allowed to interrupt the vertical rhythm.

$$ a_\mathrm{opt} = - \eta \times \frac{g - w w^\top g}{\lVert g - w w^\top g \rVert_2}. $$

Lists

Lists are square-bulleted, tight, and lead with a bold phrase when the items are parallel claims:

  • Modularity. Constraints can be mixed and matched across layers.
  • Numerics. A constraint also constrains the range of each weight entry.
  • Geometry. Operator norms do not always come from inner products.

Ordered lists are used for procedures, and nest with lower-alpha labels:

  1. Find the tangent vector of unit length that goes furthest in the gradient direction.
  2. Multiply by the learning rate and subtract from the weights.
    1. Measure length in the chosen norm.
    2. Project the result back onto the manifold.
  3. Retract the updated weights.

Quotation

A blockquote is a 2 px rule and an inset, never a coloured panel:

The reason to take steps in the tangent space is that we would like to equate the learning rate with the actual length of the optimization step.

An epigraph is the same idea, italic, with a heavier rule and no quotation marks:

A manifold is a curved surface that looks flat when you zoom in close enough.

Accents appear inside authored figures and syntax highlighting. They are never used for headings, links, buttons, callouts or backgrounds — the only exception is the red of inline code, which is a syntax colour.

Tables

The essay table is not a grid. It reads as rows of text separated by hairlines: no vertical rules, no header fill, a muted regular header, and a single hairline under every row but the last. It uses the sans at caption size so it stays subordinate to the prose.

Figures & code

A figure is full-measure, centred, unframed, and captioned underneath in 13 px sans. Captions are numbered automatically; the number is 500 weight and the rest is 400. Nothing gets a border, a shadow, or a rounded corner.

Code is the one place where a filled surface is allowed. Blocks are 13 px mono in a muted ink on #F3F3F2, with an optional language chip. Inline snippets stay transparent.

# map the gradient back to the tangent space
def retract(w, g, eta):
    direction = g - (w @ g) * w
    direction = direction / direction.norm()
    return (w - eta * direction).normalize()

Plain text is the default; pass data-lang to name a language. There is no copy button in the static template.

Components

The interface is deliberately thin. Apart from the theme toggle in the header, there are no buttons, no cards, no badges and no callouts. What exists is described below, and every example is live.

Links — rest and hover

A link at rest is muted ink with a faint underline. The same link in its hover state darkens to ink 2 and the rule strengthens. Nothing moves, nothing colours.

Theme toggle — light / dark

One icon button in the header, left of the menu. Light mode shows a moon (switch to dark), dark mode a sun. The choice is stored in localStorage as tml-theme and applied to <html> before first paint, so there is no flash; until the reader picks a side it follows prefers-color-scheme. The palette lives in the dark block of tokens.css.

Marginalia are not a component you place — they are a note you attach. The number increments automatically and the note floats into the right rail, aligning with the line that cites it.Below 768 px the note hides and the number becomes a tap target.

Header, footer, title block

Those are not reproduced in miniature — they are this page. The bar above is the header, the block at the top is the title/byline pattern, and the footer below is the footer. The reading rail at the left is the table of contents.

Motion

Movement is almost invisible. The page fades in once; links shift colour; the contents dot slides; the header retreats when you scroll down. There are no reveals, no parallax, and no animation on scroll.

Rules of the house

  • DoKeep prose serif and apparatus sans. The tension between the two is the design.
  • DoLet space do the structuring. A 3.1 rem gap above a section is stronger than a rule or a background.
  • DoUse one hairline where a border would be tempting, and no border where a hairline is not needed.
  • DoPut notes in the margin. Interrupting the column is cheaper than interrupting the sentence.
  • Don'tWiden the reading column past 660 px, or fill it with a two-column layout.
  • Don'tIntroduce rounded cards, shadows, gradients or coloured callouts. None exist in the system.
  • Don'tSet a paragraph in the sans, or a caption in the serif.
  • Don'tColour headings or links. Accent belongs to figures and syntax.
  • Don'tBold a whole sentence. Bold is for the term being defined.

Everything above is driven by tokens.css and theme.css. The rendered essay, with all of these elements in use, is at index.html.
